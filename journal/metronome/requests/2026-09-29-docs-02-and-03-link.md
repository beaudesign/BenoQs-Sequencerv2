# Request to Scribe: bring `docs/02` and `docs/03` in line with the command ring and snapshot

From: Metronome. To: Scribe (`docs/**`). Task: WENGE-0006 (O6), PR 7.
Source of truth until you have changed them: `specs/SPEC-0001/o6-test-plan.md` (sections 2
and 10) and the code in `crates/octocore/src/{ring,triple,snapshot,wire,link}.rs`.

`docs/02-architecture.md` §2 (threading model):

1. **The triple buffer.** It says the audio thread "writes into the slot that is neither
   `published` nor `reading`, then releases it by a single `store(Release)` on `published`".
   `reading` belongs to the render thread, so the writer cannot know it, and a plain `store`
   cannot work. The buffer that was built has one atomic holding the middle slot's index and
   a dirty bit. The writer swaps its slot in, dirty, and gets a free slot back. The reader
   swaps its own slot in, clean, when the bit is set, and gets the newest slot back. Both
   are one wait-free swap. The sentence "single writer, single reader, wait-free on both
   sides" stays true. See `crates/octocore/src/triple.rs`, the module comment.
2. **The size.** "About 6 KB" is 8,408 bytes: 512 LED slots at 16 bytes are 8,192 of it.
   The field list is right, apart from that the snapshot carries `mode` and the active
   bank and page as well.
3. **The ring.** It says `crossbeam` `ArrayQueue`. Q6 chose a hand-rolled ring, tested with
   loom, and it is an overwrite ring: the producer never reads anything the consumer writes,
   and a lapped consumer skips to the oldest command still there and counts the rest as
   dropped. That is the "oldest is dropped, a counter increments" policy, kept. Say that
   there is no `unsafe` in `octocore` (the slots are atomic words), so §3's statement that
   `unsafe` lives only in `octoffi` and the renderer still holds.
4. **When commands apply, and how many.** At the start of `Engine::render`, in order, before
   any tick of that call, at most 256 per call (`MAX_COMMANDS_PER_RENDER`). The snapshot is
   published at the end of every `render` call.
5. **§3, `Core`.** The interface shown (`Core`, `apply`, `tick`, `snapshot`) is the design;
   the code is `Engine`, with `handle_command`, `render`, `snapshot` and `open_link`. Note
   the difference or rename.
6. **§6, latency.** "Core apply, next tick: 1.3 ms" is not what the engine does. A command
   applies at the start of the next render, and the engine steps 12 ticks ahead of the audio
   (`MAX_EARLY_TICKS`), so an edit to a step that is less than 12 ticks from firing is heard
   on the following pass: about 31 ms at 120 BPM and about 12 ms at 300 BPM. This is the
   same consequence as item 2 of `2026-09-29-docs-03-event-enum-and-timing.md`.

`docs/03-sequencer-core.md` §5 (interfaces):

7. `Command` has two more variants after `LoadState`: `SetTrack { track: u8, attr:
   TrackAttr, value: i32 }` and `SetStep { track: u8, step: u8, attr: StepAttr, value: i32 }`.
   They write one attribute of the active page, clamped to the attribute's range, and do
   nothing for a track of 10 or more or a step of 16 or more. `size_of::<Command>()` is
   still 24 bytes. The attribute tables are `TrackAttr` and `StepAttr` in `types.rs`.
8. `RenderContext::playing` is applied at the top of the render and overrides `Command::Play`,
   `Stop` and `HostTransport`. This is recorded in `tests/conformance/AMBIGUITIES.md`; it is
   what O4 will settle, so say "as built" and point at that entry.
