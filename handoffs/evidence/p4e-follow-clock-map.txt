WHAT IT MEANS. The follower reads the engine's place through the same map the scheduler stamps the notes with, so a jump in the map moves the
notes' timestamps too: that is a property of the browser's clock for audio and not of the follower. Whether a real device's output timestamp jumps is
not measured here; the owner's S1 run (clock-map smoothing is its alpha option) and S3 run will say. The S3 page now records the map at every sample
(mapMs), and the analysis counts the jumps and says how many samples beyond a fifth of a step follow one, so a run with a real sender can tell the
browser's clock from the sender's. The Chromium test no longer claims every sample is within a fifth of a step: it holds the median, and that each
sample beyond a fifth follows a jump of the map, which fails if an excursion has no jump before it.
