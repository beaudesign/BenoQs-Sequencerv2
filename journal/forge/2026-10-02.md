## 2026-10-02, later: P4d

Task WENGE-0015, SPEC-0002 P4d. Hat: **Forge**, with a **Conductor** hat for ADR-0009 amendment 2 (the prefix on that commit says so). Built on P4c (PR #31), so
it carries P4a's, P4b's and P4c's commits and the plan's (merge #27, #29, #30, #31, then this). No Rust, contract, golden or harness file changed.

**Intend to change:** make the page a master, a slave, or a slave that echoes the clock: an estimator that reads the pulses of an incoming clock, a follower that steers
the engine's tempo and transport from outside, the echo, and the clock state in the strip with its sentences.
**How I'll know it worked:** the plan's M4 and M5 hold against a simulated sender and, with the real engine in the loop, at the device; M7 and M8 hold in Chromium for all
four states; a deliberate breakage of each new behaviour fails a test.

### What happened

- **Slice A, the estimator** (`src/clock-estimator.ts`): a two-state Kalman filter with adaptive process noise. 28 fixtures red against a stub first (23 of 25 failed at the
  first count, the passing ones named), then green. **Defect found by the follower's fixtures:** through a step in tempo the pulse count went wrong in 42 of 100 runs; an
  anomaly record, a check of each pulse against the raw spacing and one `relock()` took it to 0 of 100 (`p4d-estimator-measured.txt`).
- **Slice B, the follower** (`src/follower.ts`) and the worklet's position (`src/worklet.ts`, `src/host.ts`): a first-order loop on the engine's tempo, a lead of the
  lookahead plus a user offset, Start and Continue at the next pulse, Stop at once, a stall says so and sends nothing. 28 failing fixtures before it, the seven that
  passed by construction named. **Defect found only by measuring on seeds the tests did not use:** the harness put the Start after the first pulse to arrive in 18 of 40 runs,
  and every one of those settled a whole pulse off. A port keeps its messages in order, so that stream cannot happen; the harness was wrong, not the follower.
- **Slice C, the clock state, the echo and the page** (`src/clock-state.ts`, `MidiScheduler.passThrough`, `pages/app.ts`, the strip): 15 of 16 clock-state tests, 6 of 6
  echo tests and 13 of 41 page tests were red before their code (`p4d-clock-red.txt`). Then **the follower in front of the real engine**
  (`test/follower-engine.test.ts`, `test/support/engine-loop.ts`): 9 tests, measured at the device.
- **Slice D, wrap-up:** mutation runs (`p4d-mutation-run.txt`): 35 on the estimator and follower, 34 on the rest. Three survivors of the second run each got an assertion;
  three of the first remain and are named (two redundant guards that die as a pair, one equivalent). ADR-0009 amendment 2.
- **Gates:** tsc clean; 277 Node tests (178 at P4c); 50 Chromium tests (40 at P4c); the Rust source rules 16 + 4; `just verify-scope` pass.

### What I learned and what surprised me

- **A flaky P4c test, explained.** The Chromium clock test saw a first pulse gap of 32, 41 or 53 ms where 20.8 was expected, in 2 of 15 runs here and 1 of 25 at P4c's own
  commit. At the start of an AudioContext `currentFrame` jumps 1024 frames between the first and second `process()` call; the engine renders only the calls it gets,
  so a Play in that window has a hole of about 20 ms. The tests that measure spacing now wait for the panel to have been sent eight times (0 failures in 30 runs after).
  `scripts/measure-startup-frames.ts`: 10 of 12 fresh contexts jump. **This is headless Chromium on a build container with no sound device**; whether a real device does it,
  and whether `process()` can skip mid-session (which would leave the engine behind the audio timeline for good), I have not measured and cannot here.
- **My fake sender was wrong.** It asked a timer for 90 BPM and made 92.6: a timer given a fractional interval cuts it to whole milliseconds. The test that exposed it was
  right to fail. The sender now times each pulse from the start.
- **The notes are 11 ticks behind the pulses, still.** My first notes test expected them on the pulse and read 10.8 ticks; I had forgotten my own note in `clock.rs`. The
  follower lands the engine's *pulses* on the sender's grid; the notes of a pattern sit 11 ticks after it. Not a follower fault, and D0's owner decision still stands.
- **The honest number at 160 BPM.** With the output's own timing noise on, a tick is not reliably held at 160 BPM (worst 1.11 of 1.9 ms). I did not write a test whose bound
  would not hold and did not turn the noise off to make the figure pass: the tests that hold the follower alone say the noise is off, and the evidence says what it is on.
- **Passing by construction, again.** One clock-state test passes against the stub; it is named in the red evidence and the mutation run shows it can fail.

### Departures and defaults, flagged

- **ADR-0009 amendment 2**, six items: a first-order loop and not a second-order one; a stateful estimator; the sign of the lead; Start, Continue and Stop at the next pulse and
  now, with no song position; **the echo is sent at once and not stamped ahead**; the clock state and its refusals.
- **Defaults taken for the owner to overturn:** the local Play key is not gated while slaved; the tempo knob is overwritten by the sender's tempo. Both are in the ADR.
- **Thresholds are mine** (M4 and D-P4-10): none loosened; the first drafts of the steady and catch-up bounds were tightened; the ramp bound of 4 ticks has a margin of
  0.49 and nine seeds of twenty are over 3.
- **M7 and M8 are now met in full**, in the page; **M3 stays pending on D0**.

## 2026-10-02, later: P4e

Task WENGE-0015, SPEC-0002 P4e. Hat: **Forge**. Built on P4d; the owner merged #32 at 14:52 Paris while this was being written, and main is merged into this branch, so the diff is P4e's own. No Rust, contract, golden or

**Intend to change:** give the owner a way to measure how well the page follows Live's clock (spike S3) and a guide to using the app with Live, and run what can be run here, said to be

**How I'll know it worked:** the analysis tests (written first, red) say what a clock domain, an interval, a phase against a fifth of a step and a loopback pairing are; a Chromium test
drives the page's own buttons through a follow run and a send run and reads the saved file back; the runner makes a complete record with the real engine, worklet, scheduler and follower
and a stand-in clock; a deliberate breakage of each piece fails a test.

### What happened

- **Slice A, the analysis** (`spikes/s3/analyse.ts`): clock domain (is `event.timeStamp` on `performance.now()`'s clock: a stamp a little behind the time it was seen is, one in the
  future or far behind is not), intervals (tempo, scatter, drift in BPM an hour from 60 s blocks, stalls, stamps going backwards, equal stamps, bursts), phase against a fifth of a
  step (counted only while the transport runs and the clock is not lost, after 3 s of settling), the loop pairing by order, and the verdict as a sentence. 22 tests, red against a
  stub first (`p4e-s3-red.txt`), including two arithmetic slips of my own in the tests that I found and fixed before the code.
- **Slice B, the page, report and runner** (`spikes/s3/page.ts`, `format.ts`, `report.ts`, `run.ts`, `stand-in.ts`): a follow run on a real input (the real Host and Follower), a send
  run (engine master, loopback pairing by order), saved as `wenge.s3.follow/1` / `wenge.s3.send/1` raw records; `report.ts` recomputes every figure from those records, so another
  settling or a nominal tempo needs no second 30 minutes. 9 report tests (red first, the weakest kind: a missing module, said so in `p4e-s3-report-red.txt`) and 5 Chromium tests that
  press the page's own buttons.
- **Slice C, hardening by what the first runs showed:** see "what surprised me". `ABLETON.md` rewritten (routing both ways, the two opposite-signed delay settings, the test procedure,
  known gaps), `README` row and `just spike-s3`.
- **Slice D, wrap-up:** a 53-mutant run (`p4e-mutation-run.txt`): 47 caught first, six survivors (and one doubtful), five of them answered by a test each and re-run; 51 of 53 caught, two
  that stand named with their reasons.
- **Gates (this head):** `just verify` PASS (4 pass, 0 fail, 6 not implemented; assertions 503, the baseline); tsc clean; 307 Node tests (277 at P4d); 55 Chromium tests (50 at P4d);
  Rust workspace 455 passed, 0 failed, 4 ignored. The simulated run (`p4e-s3-simulated.txt`, 60 s, `just spike-s3`): 2813 pulses, tempo 120.000, 3 of 223 phase samples beyond a fifth
  of a step, each within 2 s after a jump of the browser's clock map (the largest jump 30 ms); the send run 2881 of 2881 pulses matched, the engine at 120.000 BPM by its own audio times. The run's own verdict reads **NOT MET** (the criterion is every sample within a fifth), and says why: all three excursions follow a clock-map jump. Its header says "uncommitted changes in apps/web": that is the untracked `node_modules` link of this worktree, not a source change.

### What I learned and what surprised me

- **The browser's own clock map jumps.** `AudioContext.getOutputTimestamp`, through which the page turns audio time into page time, stepped by 7 to 43 ms between samples in 5 of 16
  ten-second headless runs (`p4e-follow-clock-map.txt`). The phase is read through that map, so a jump in it is a jump in the phase, and it is not the sender's. The page now records the
  map at each sample, the report counts the jumps and says how many excursions follow one, and the verdict says so. **This is headless Chromium with no sound device**; whether a real
  device does it is for the owner's run. No mid-session skip of the worklet in 80 s (`scripts/measure-midsession-frames.ts`: 0).
- **The send run started in Chromium's start-up hole,** the same 20 ms hole as at P4d: the Chromium test failed 3 times in 6, and in a script of fresh 6 s runs the first pulse was 135 to 151 ms late in 2 of 8 and again 2 of 8 (`p4e-send-first-pulse.txt`). The page
  waits for 8 panel messages before starting, as the spacing tests do. Not the app's fault (a person presses Start before Play); the page's.
- **A stand-in that agreed with the follower is worth little.** My first stand-in stamped each pulse with the time the timer fired, so stamp and seen were equal. It now stamps 2 ms
  behind with 0.3 ms of scatter, and the page and tests assert the age. The mutant that feeds the follower `seen` and not `stamp` still survives (`p4e-mutation-run.txt`, [51]): the
  simulation cannot tell them apart, and the real run's clock-domain line is where that is measured.
- **Smaller defects found by measuring:** the input counts read 0 (the port's stats reset when the port is released; now copied first); the fifth of a step taken from the median of the
  periods put the tempo at 117.9 (now from the run's tempo); the runner's pulse threshold ignored the sender's start delay.
- **The user asked, mid-turn, for a score out of 10** (Fri 15:40 Paris). The scorecard went out as a message (6 / 10) and is not a deliverable in the repo. One probe came out of it that is
  not in the repo: a random-input run through `pages/host.html` (4 runs of 20 s, about 11,500 clicks, tempo, transport and clock-master changes; 0 errors, 0 notes held after Stop, panel
  updates never stalled). It also showed the engine counting 300 to 500 "dropped intents" per run: those are panel presses the engine has no capability behind yet (the Audition intent, per
  `engine/tests/abi.rs`), counted and not silent, not a ring overflow.
