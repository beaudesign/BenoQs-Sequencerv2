## What I did

- Read benny's README, licence, changelog, the `help_text` of its source, voice and filter blocks, and the names (and, for
  some, the function outline) of its code files. It is Max/MSP patches under the Hippocratic License 3.0, which has a
  notice duty and a copyleft clause, and this repository has no licence file. So the spec takes **nothing** from benny
  but the idea of a block, and implements each piece from the paper it points at.
- Read the monitor and the worklet. Today's "piano" is a sine and two harmonics with a 60 ms fall, and the sound is
  audio only. Its header calls it "deliberately plain": the spec says this changes on purpose (D1).
- Planned seven slices (S0 to S6) from a Voice interface and an offline WAV renderer to four voices, with fifteen
  observables that measure what makes a circuit sound like itself: alias level, pitch, filter slope and ring, key
  follow, RC envelopes, the low-pass gate's uneven fall, cost.

## What I learned

- benny is not a library to port. Its gen code is partly third-party (an oscillator from another site, filter models by
  Surreal Machines) and its best oscillator uses the author's own hardware recordings. The honest route is the papers.
- A demo that already puts bass and lead on channels 1 and 2 means a voice per MIDI channel (S5) gives "oscillator types
  played through the sequencer" without any change to the engine or a panel control.

## What surprised me

- While surveying benny I opened a file that holds its voice allocator, which this plan does not need. It is written in the
  spec so that no one wonders; nothing from it is used.

## What I left out on purpose

- Any code. Effects. A bounce or recording path. Stereo. Any change to the drums. Any choice of licence.
