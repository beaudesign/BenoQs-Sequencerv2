### What I learned

- The previous layout test asserted a premise that was false: that a layout of any grid was the Octopus. The 16 tests I replaced asserted the old grid; they were not weakened, their premise was removed, and the new set
  is larger (26 layout tests plus 10 Chromium tests) and asserts positions against measured numbers.
- Identity is the hard part, not geometry. The photograph's printed names are about 4 px high. 63 controls are read from it; 18 are a stated order within a group (ten mutators top to bottom, seven chords, the 200 key); one
  (`mode.edit`) by position. They are marked `basis: order` in the file so that the first person with an Octopus can correct them with one line each.
- Tab order is a cost of 242 tab stops. The reading order is one matrix row high, left to right, so a keyboard reader follows the plate; the README says so and names the cost.

### What surprised me

- The photograph shows an LED beside Play, Stop and Pause. Q26 (do the transport keys have LEDs?) was open; the photograph answers it with a dot, so they are drawn as dead dots, which the engine will not light.
- The plate has a row of eleven MIX TARGET keys (ATR VOL PAN MOD EXP MAP 1 to 5) and a row of ten number keys that are in no inventory. The EDIT column bows by about 1.5 pitches. None of this is in `controls.json`.
- My first lattice fit assumed the matrix's first column at x of about 203 px; it is at about 170. Refitting gave all 160 keys a residual under 0.9 px. The script now asserts 160 distinct lattice cells and prints the residual with every run.
