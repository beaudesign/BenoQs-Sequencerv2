# P4d follower: what it measured, and what changed between the red run and the green one
# command: node apps/web/scripts/measure-follower.ts   (the sender and the engine are made up: test/support/sim-clock.ts and plant.ts; nothing
# here was measured on a real port, a real sender or a real browser. The real engine is in the loop in test/follower-engine.test.ts, slice C.)
#
# Defects found while the follower was made green. Two of them were found only by measuring on seeds the tests do not use (100 to 139):
#  1. The pulse count went wrong through a step in tempo (the estimator's: see p4d-estimator-measured.txt, defect 4). The follower's own
#     phase read zero while the heard error was a whole pulse, which is what a count that is one out looks like. First seen as the
#     failing "step in tempo (120 to 90 BPM)" test (8.23 ticks on seed 1); a loop over five more steps (up, down, 1.5 times, double, half)
#     is the fixture that was added and stayed red against the old counting.
#  2. THE HARNESS PUT THE START AFTER THE FIRST PULSE. The loop sent 0xFA a millisecond before the sender's first pulse as the sender made
#     it, but the pulses carry jitter, and in 18 of 40 steady runs the first pulse to arrive had come earlier than that: the Start was
#     heard after it, the first clock after the Start was the sender's SECOND pulse, and the engine's first pulse was taken to be the
#     sender's second. Every such run settled a whole pulse (8 ticks) off. A port keeps its messages in order, so that stream cannot
#     happen; the fault was the harness's, not the follower's. The Start now goes one millisecond before the first pulse to ARRIVE. The
#     tests on seeds 1 to 12 had not hit it (it needs the first pulse early by more than a millisecond). Mutation "Start before the sender's
#     first pulse and not the first to arrive" is in the run recorded in p4d-mutation-run.txt.
#  3. A test window was measured from Start while its event was in page time: the "lead can be changed" test read the sample at the moment
#     of the change (a whole offset away from the new target, by construction) as "steady before". Fixed by putting the windows in page
#     time, and the test now asserts the sample at the moment of the change IS far from the target (so the change is a real one).
#
# Thresholds. None was loosened. Two were TIGHTENED once the numbers were in (the first draft said 1.5 ticks steady and 2 ticks for the
# catch-up; the plan says "within one tick"): steady and after-a-step are now 1 tick (measured worst 0.59 on 40 unseen seeds at 160 BPM),
# the catch-up from 1.5 s after the Start is now 1 tick (worst 0.69). The ramp bounds (3 and 4 ticks) are the first draft's: the slow ramp
# measured 2.07 and the fast ramp 3.78 on the ramp itself, which is close to the 4 and says the loop does not have much room there:
# a ramp at 5 BPM a second is followed with a lag the estimator and the loop share (the estimator alone: 2.14 ticks).
#
# Restated, not met as the plan words it: after a Stop and a Continue the engine resumes where it stopped (it does not rewind, and song
# position 0xF2 is decoded and ignored), so the test measures the FINE placement (modulo a whole pulse) from two seconds after the Continue.
# A sender that Continues from the middle of a song does not get its song position from this follower: that is said in follower.ts, in
# ABLETON.md (slice P4e), and is not tested because there is nothing to test.
#
# What these tests do not say: whether Web MIDI's event.timeStamp is on performance.now()'s clock (assumed; spike S3 measures it), how
# the audio-to-page clock map moves in a real browser (made up here as 1.5 ms of jitter on the position's time), and what a real sender's
# jitter looks like (uniform plus or minus 3 ms here, which is the plan's number).
#

# SLICE C: the follower in front of the REAL engine (the engine, worklet, scheduler and follower are real; the sender's pulses and the receiver are made up)
# command: node apps/web/scripts/measure-follower-engine.ts   (about a minute). Measured at the DEVICE: when each engine pulse is delivered, against the
# sender's pulse with the same number. The tests use seeds 1 to 7; these are seeds 100 to 119.
#
# What it says, in words:
#  * With the output's own timing noise off (what is left is the follower, the engine and the noise of the sender and of the position map), the
#    worst heard error is under half a tick in steady state at 70, 120 and 160 BPM, after a step in tempo and after a ramp. The count is right: a
#    pulse out of place would read 8 ticks. On a ramp of 3.75 BPM a second the worst is 3.51 ticks (p50 2.96): the bound is 4, the number the
#    simulated follower's fast ramp has, and nine seeds of twenty are over 3, so the margin is thin and nobody should call it comfortable.
#  * With the output noise ON (the page's idea of the audio clock wrong by up to 1.5 ms at each batch of events) the figures are mostly that noise:
#    120 BPM stays under a tick (0.85), 160 BPM does not (1.11; 5 of 20 over), because a tick there is 1.9 ms. The test of the bound at 120 BPM with the
#    noise on is kept; there is no such test at 160 BPM because the bound would not hold, and that is said here and not hidden by dropping the noise.
#  * The two noises are drawn independently here. If the page's map is wrong in the same way for the position it reports and the events it stamps
#    (they share one ContextTimeMap in the app) the follower will see and cancel part of it; or it may be worse. Unknown until spike S3.
#  * The engine's own notes sit 11 ticks after the pulse of their step (crates/octocore/tests/clock.rs, AMBIGUITIES.md "the first note after Play"): a fact
#    of the engine and a question for the owner, found again here when a first draft of the notes test expected them on the pulse and read 10.8 ticks.
